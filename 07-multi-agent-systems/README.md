
# 07 — Multi-Agent Systems

## 🎯 Objective

The objective of this experiment is to understand how multiple specialized Large Language Model (LLM) agents can collaborate to solve a business intelligence problem.

Instead of relying on a single LLM to perform every task, the experiment distributes responsibilities across three agents:

- Analyst
- Strategist
- Reviewer

A Python orchestrator coordinates their execution, manages the information exchanged between agents, and controls the workflow.

The experiment focuses on:

- Understanding the role of specialized AI agents
- Coordinating multiple agents through a Python application
- Sharing context and results between agents
- Combining LLM reasoning with deterministic Python calculations
- Generating structured business recommendations
- Reviewing and validating the output
- Understanding the benefits and limitations of multi-agent systems

## 🏗️ Architecture

The experiment introduces a multi-agent architecture in which each agent has a specific responsibility.

The agents do not operate independently. A Python orchestrator controls their execution and transfers the information required at each stage.

The architecture is composed of five main components:

```text
User
 ↓
Business Objective
 ↓
Python Orchestrator
 ↓
Analytics Tools
 ↓
Verified Business Metrics
 ↓
Analyst
 ↓
Business Analysis
 ↓
Strategist
 ↓
Structured Recommendations
 ↓
Reviewer
 ↓
Python Validator
 ↓
Final Report
```

### Components

#### LLM

Qwen3 8B runs locally through Ollama.

The model is used by all three agents, each with a different system prompt and responsibility.

The LLM is responsible for interpreting information, generating recommendations, and evaluating their quality.

It does not replace the deterministic calculations performed by Python.

---

#### Python Orchestrator

The orchestrator is implemented in:

```text
07-multi-agent-systems/src/orchestrator.py
```

It coordinates the complete workflow.

Its responsibilities include:

- Loading the business dataset
- Calculating verified metrics
- Invoking the Analyst
- Passing the analysis to the Strategist
- Sending recommendations to the Reviewer
- Running deterministic validation
- Requesting revisions when necessary
- Producing the final report

The orchestrator also manages workflow states, errors, and a maximum of two revision attempts.

---

#### Analyst

The Analyst is implemented in:

```text
07-multi-agent-systems/src/agents/analyst.py
```

Its purpose is to interpret business metrics and identify relevant patterns.

The Analyst receives:

- Company-level metrics
- Product-level metrics
- Product rankings calculated by Python

It identifies revenue and gross profit contributors, interprets differences between sales volume and margins, and highlights potential business opportunities.

The numerical rankings are calculated by Python rather than generated by the model.

---

#### Strategist

The Strategist is implemented in:

```text
07-multi-agent-systems/src/agents/strategist.py
```

Its purpose is to transform the Analyst's findings into actionable business recommendations.

The Strategist receives the business objective, the Analyst's findings, and verified reference data.

It returns structured JSON containing:

- Recommendation
- Priority
- Supporting metrics
- Rationale
- Assumptions

This structure allows the Python application to inspect the metrics referenced by each recommendation.

---

#### Reviewer

The Reviewer is implemented in:

```text
07-multi-agent-systems/src/agents/reviewer.py
```

Its purpose is to evaluate the quality and reliability of the recommendations.

It checks whether recommendations address the objective, use supported numerical claims, identify assumptions, and provide actionable reasoning.

The Reviewer returns a structured assessment containing an approval decision, issues, and feedback.

The Reviewer is itself an LLM. Its approval is a qualitative assessment and does not guarantee complete factual correctness.

---

#### Analytics and Validation Tools

The deterministic tools are implemented in:

```text
07-multi-agent-systems/src/tools/analytics.py
07-multi-agent-systems/src/tools/validator.py
```

The analytics module calculates:

- Revenue
- Total cost
- Gross profit
- Gross margin percentage
- Total units sold
- Product rankings

The validator checks the structure of the recommendations and compares their supporting metric references with verified business facts.

This creates a separation between numerical calculations performed by Python and interpretation performed by the LLM agents.

## 🔧 Implementation

### Business Dataset

The experiment uses a synthetic dataset representing TechStore's sales during Q3 2026.

The dataset is stored in:

```text
07-multi-agent-systems/data.json
```

It contains six products:

```text
Laptop
Smartphone
Monitor
Headphones
Keyboard
Mouse
```

The available data includes units sold, unit price, unit cost, product category, and currency.

The dataset is illustrative and does not represent actual company performance.

---

### Deterministic Analytics

Before invoking the agents, Python calculates the business metrics from the source data.

For each product:

```text
Units Sold × Unit Price
          ↓
        Revenue
          ↓
Revenue − Total Cost
          ↓
     Gross Profit
          ↓
Gross Profit / Revenue
          ↓
   Margin Percentage
```

Python also calculates rankings by revenue, gross profit, margin percentage, and units sold.

These rankings are provided to the Analyst as verified reference information.

This reduces the need for the LLM to perform arithmetic or reconstruct numerical rankings independently.

---

### Agent Workflow

The complete workflow follows these steps:

```text
1. Load source data
        ↓
2. Calculate verified metrics
        ↓
3. Build product rankings
        ↓
4. Analyst interprets the data
        ↓
5. Strategist generates recommendations
        ↓
6. Reviewer evaluates recommendations
        ↓
7. Python validates supporting metrics
        ↓
8. Check approval and validation results
        ↓
   Approved and valid?
      /       \
    Yes        No
     ↓          ↓
 Final Report  Request Revision
                  ↓
             Repeat Checks
```

The workflow requests a revision when the Reviewer rejects the recommendations or the deterministic validator detects an issue.

The maximum number of revision attempts is two.

If the workflow cannot obtain approval with valid supporting metrics, it finishes with the `needs_review` status rather than treating the result as fully approved.

---

### Structured Recommendations

The Strategist returns JSON rather than unstructured text alone.

An example of the expected structure is:

```json
{
  "recommendations": [
    {
      "recommendation": "Review the pricing strategy for Mouse.",
      "priority": 1,
      "supporting_metrics": [
        {
          "product": "Mouse",
          "metric": "margin_percentage",
          "value": 65.71
        }
      ],
      "rationale": "The product has the highest gross margin percentage.",
      "assumptions": []
    }
  ]
}
```

The Python validator checks whether the referenced product exists and whether the metric value matches the verified data.

This is a deterministic check of structured metric references, not a complete verification of every natural-language claim.

## 🧪 Experiment 01 — End-to-End Multi-Agent Workflow

### Objective

Identify the most profitable products and recommend actions to improve business profitability.

### Execution

The application was executed locally using:

```bash
python3 07-multi-agent-systems/src/main.py
```

The system loaded the synthetic sales dataset, calculated the metrics, and invoked the three agents sequentially.

### Observation

The Analyst identified the main contributors to revenue and gross profit and distinguished them from products with the highest margin percentage.

The Strategist generated three prioritized recommendations focused on:

- Increasing sales of the Mouse
- Improving the performance of the Keyboard
- Maintaining Smartphone revenue while exploring margin improvements

The Reviewer approved the recommendations.

The deterministic validator reported no inconsistencies in the supporting metric references.

No revision attempts were required.

### Result

The workflow completed successfully with the status:

```text
completed
```

The experiment demonstrated that specialized agents can collaborate through a Python orchestrator to produce a structured business intelligence report.

## 🔎 Key Findings

Multi-Agent Systems allow a complex task to be divided among agents with different responsibilities.

The system demonstrated that agents can:

- Interpret verified business metrics
- Generate recommendations based on an analysis
- Return structured outputs
- Evaluate another agent's work
- Participate in a workflow managed by Python
- Request revisions when validation or review identifies issues

### Core Principle

> Specialized agents divide the reasoning task, while the orchestrator controls the workflow and Python provides deterministic calculations and validation.

### Benefits

- **Specialization:** Each agent has a clearly defined role.
- **Separation of concerns:** Analysis, strategy, and review are handled independently.
- **Shared context:** Agents receive information from earlier stages.
- **Structured communication:** JSON makes recommendations easier to inspect programmatically.
- **Deterministic calculations:** Python provides a consistent source for numerical metrics and rankings.
- **Workflow control:** The orchestrator manages execution, errors, and revision attempts.

### Limitations

The experiment also highlighted several limitations.

- Multiple agents do not automatically produce more accurate results than a single agent.
- The Reviewer can approve recommendations even when the analysis contains unsupported statements.
- The current validator checks structured supporting metrics but does not verify every numerical claim in free text.
- Business recommendations may be plausible without being empirically demonstrated by the available data.
- The dataset does not include operating expenses, customer acquisition costs, market share, or competitor information.
- Sequential local inference can produce significant latency.

The experiment therefore demonstrates a functional cooperative architecture, not a production-ready business intelligence system.

---

## 🔐 Reliability Considerations

The experiment combines two different mechanisms:

**LLM-based evaluation**

The Reviewer assesses reasoning, relevance, assumptions, and recommendation quality.

**Deterministic validation**

Python checks whether structured metric references match the verified business data.

These mechanisms serve different purposes.

The Reviewer can evaluate qualitative aspects that are difficult to express as simple numerical rules. Python can verify specific numerical references without relying on another model's judgment.

Neither mechanism, in its current implementation, guarantees that every statement in the final report is correct.
